N117ZS governor hunting simulator

Rotax 916iS with Airmaster AP431 and AC300 controller, Takeoff mode at full throttle. The engine and propeller model is fitted to the aircraft's flight logs, and the governor rules are decoded from the controller's 50 Hz diagnostic log.

Governor logic is inferred from logs. It is not Airmaster firmware, and its predictions for untested settings need a flight test.

Assumptions, method and results are set out in the accompanying paper.

    Log figures are medians from the controller's 50 Hz record: 46 Takeoff segments on 10.139 and one on 10.170 (11 Nov 2026). Model figures are computed in this page when it loads. Climb at 5400 rpm is steady in both the logs and the model.

    What it shows, and limits

    • At full throttle MAP rises from 43 to 50 inHg between 5527 and 5765 rpm. Engine power then grows with RPM about as fast as propeller load, so RPM barely settles on its own.
    • On 10.139 a plain deadband of about ±58 rpm hunts on that ramp. With the ramp removed it is steady.
    • On 10.170 the overspeed logic begins 100 rpm below Par 511. With the target at 5700 it begins at the target, so the upper half of the deadband is gone. It still hunts with the ramp removed.
    • Proposed change 1 removes the rule that takes away the upper deadband. Change 2 adds rate damping to the existing logic. Change 3 replaces the on/off deadband with a continuous drive. Rate of change is already computed by the firmware.
    • Switching to Climb mode moves the target to 5400 rpm, below the ramp and clear of the overspeed zone. The hunting stops with the throttle untouched, as in flight.
    • Fitted to two firmware versions near sea level. No altitude, temperature or airspeed effects, no ground roll, no throttle transients.
    • Pitch is in controller units. The decoded rules match the logged motor state on 96 to 97% of samples.
